# Solution: pass@k and pass^k

pass@3 answers whether one of three attempts is good, but nobody reviews these attempts: every sent followup ships, so the honest metric is pass^k, the probability that all attempts in a row are good. At the stand-in's boundary rates that number is near 0.11 at k of 3, not 0.99. I would sign when pass^k at the k matching a realistic unsupervised run is measured, with its trial count stated, at a level the business accepts for unreviewed customer contact, or when a human review step is added that makes pass@k the honest metric.
